Internships ranked on proof

Get hired on what you can prove.

Draft turns an engineering student's resume into skills with the evidence behind each one, then scores applicants on weights the employer publishes. Students show what they can back up. Employers read fewer, better resumes.

Value proposition

In one sentence

Draft helps engineering students and the employers who hire them who want to land and fill internships by putting proof behind every skill and ranking applicants on weights everyone can see.

Unlike keyword resume filters and job boards, which reward wording over ability and hide how a ranking was made.

The student's problem

Every resume says "Python." Students cannot show which claims are real, so a strong project looks the same as a keyword. Many apply nervously and never learn why they were passed over.

The employer's problem

A single posting can draw a thousand near-identical resumes. Filters that match exact words reward the right phrasing, not the right skills, and good candidates get lost.

How it works

Resume in. Proof attached. Ranked in the open.

STEP 1

Upload a resume

A PDF, a Word file or a photo of a printed page. Draft reads it, proposes skills, and keeps only the text lines so you can see what was read.

STEP 2

Attach the proof

Link a project or a public repository, or pass a short timed skill check. Each skill shows how it was backed up, and the student chooses what to keep.

STEP 3

Rank on published weights

The employer sets a weight for each skill the role needs. The score is computed with one formula that both sides can read, and a person reviews before anyone is advanced or passed.

The score

Three levels of proof, told apart at a glance

The level is worked out on the server from what is attached. It is never typed in, so a student cannot award themselves a higher one.

Claimed0.45

The student says they have the skill. A tick, nothing more.

Evidenced0.70

There is a link an employer can open: a project, a repository, a write-up.

Verified1.00

Passed a skill check, or confirmed through the student's own public GitHub work.

For students

  • See exactly what an employer will see.
  • Know which single step would raise your score.
  • Suggestions reuse your own words and leave a bracket where a number belongs. Draft never invents experience.

For employers

  • Post a role and weight the skills it needs.
  • Work a ranked queue instead of reading everything.
  • Advance and Pass stay locked until a person has reviewed.

Draft works alongside Handshake and a campus career centre. It does not try to replace them.
