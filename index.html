About the founder

I'm building Draft because I'm the customer it's for.

I've built a ROS 2 food delivery robot that reads signs with vision-only machine learning, programmed MSP430 microcontrollers register by register, and designed digital logic in Verilog on an FPGA. On a one-page resume, each of those becomes a single bullet that a recruiter looks at for a fraction of a second. Every semester I watch classmates with impressive builds send applications into piles of a hundred or more and never hear back.

So I started building the fix myself in August 2026. The Draft alpha already reads a resume from a PDF or a phone photo, proposes skills with the exact line of evidence behind each one, and verifies them through GitHub or a timed skill check. The next step is a pilot with my own classmates in UCF's College of Engineering and Computer Science, 15,500 students strong.

Offerings

Proof up front, no more guessing

Four things Draft gives students, and the same features give employers trustworthy skill signals and fewer, better-matched applicants.

For students

A profile built on proof

Your best work sits at the top: source code, CAD renders, schematics, demo video and test data. Every skill shows how it was proven, so a recruiter knows in seconds what to trust.

  • Upload a resume PDF or a phone photo; Draft proposes skills with the line it found
  • Three levels: Claimed, Evidenced, Verified
  • Verify through Sign in with GitHub or a short timed skill check
For students

Always know where you stand

Every application has a live timeline with dates, like a package tracker. "Reviewed" means a person actually opened it, and every posting commits to a decide-by date.

  • Applied, Reviewed, Advanced or Passed, Decision
  • Missed decision dates close the application and lower the employer's responsiveness score
  • Verified employers and real, open roles only
For students

Apply where you fit

Employers publish how much each skill matters for a role. You see your match score and the breakdown before you apply, so you send six strong applications instead of forty hopeful ones.

  • Match score with a skill-by-skill breakdown
  • The one skill that would raise your score most
  • One profile link to share on Handshake and LinkedIn too
For employers

A ranked queue you can trust

Each applicant arrives with a match score and a breakdown of how every skill was proven. Advance and Pass stay locked until someone has reviewed the application, and reply templates let a small team answer hundreds of applicants in a few clicks.

  • Ranked on proven skills, never on payment
  • Verified company badge
  • Reply templates that end ghosting
Pricing

Free for students. Employers pay only when they hire.

One flat fee per hire, tiered by company size and never a percentage of the intern's pay. The average fee of about $1,500 is roughly a quarter of the $5,475 employers already spend per hire (SHRM 2025).
